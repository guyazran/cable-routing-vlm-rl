Cable routing in simulation: Franka robot and new clip layouts

The floating gripper is replaced by a Franka Emika Panda, as in the paper's simulation media. Each video shows the whole robot and every clip on the left, and a close-up of the clip being worked on on the right. Three new layout families need sharper turns and longer moves between clips than the trail-like study family. The controller is the ground-truth reference controller with the heuristic stand-in insertion policy, because no RL checkpoint has been trained yet. The VLM is not involved.

What changed and what to keep in mind

  • The arm follows the existing grasp body, so physics is unchanged. Before every rendered frame, IK places the Panda's fingertips on the kinematic grasp body (top-down, closing axis = gripper yaw); the arm has no colliders or joints in PhysX. FK matches the Isaac asset to 0.4 µm. A static box at the pedestal keeps the cable out of the base.
  • Free-space moves are shown, not simulated. The executor teleports the gripper between grasps because those moves cannot touch the cable. The video interpolates them around the base, and the fingers open and close over 0.3 s. Every cable-holding motion is simulated step by step and played in real time (before the page's speed-up).
  • Observation images are unchanged. The planner's top-down image and crops hide the arm, as a wrist camera at the observation pose would not see it.
  • Reach. A real Panda reaches 0.20-0.78 m top-down at table height. The new families are generated inside that band. The study family (primary10) uses a 0.15-0.90 m reach model, and none of its development scenes fits fully inside the real reach once the 16 cm insertion regions are counted. In the primary10 video the hand is up to 23 mm off the grasp body at moments when the skills ask for a grasp outside the arm's reach (the IK returns the closest pose); elsewhere it is exact.
  • Controller. The reference controller with the heuristic insertion stand-in, not the trained RL policy or the VLM. Failures on the new families show where these scripted skills break, not where the study system would.
  • Why runs fail. Of the 30 physics-only runs that did not complete, 19 ended when the heuristic Insert's gripper overlapped a clip, 7 with a gripper-clip collision in Pull or Flatten, and 4 hit the decision cap while Insert or Flatten kept repeating. The heuristic Insert grasps the head even when the head lies against the clip, or slides its fingers past the clip at table height, and the gripper then overlaps a clip (a terminal collision under the study rule). At sharp turns Flatten often has to repeat: the bent cable springs back and leaves the head misaligned. These are the limits of the scripted stand-ins on the new layouts, and a target for the RL insertion policy.
  • Outcomes are sensitive to tiny state differences. Rendering neither steps nor perturbs physics (checked: identical cable states with and without rendering). But the first episode after a scene is loaded ends about 0.1 mm away from later replays of the same episode, which are bitwise identical to each other, and the cable dynamics amplify such differences. A video's run can therefore end differently from the physics-only run of the same scene; each video reports its own outcome.
  • Scope. The new families are development/demo families (deviation L5) and do not enter the five-vs-ten screen.
